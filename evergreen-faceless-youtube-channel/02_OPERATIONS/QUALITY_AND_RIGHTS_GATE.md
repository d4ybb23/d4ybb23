# Quality, Rights, and Monetization Gate

**Purpose:** Prevent the expensive kind of shortcut: the one that damages trust, creates a claim, or makes an episode look mass-produced.  
**Who signs:** Channel Principal; an additional reviewer is recommended for sensitive claims, sponsorships, or extensive third-party material.

## 1. The Gate

No public upload moves forward until each item is marked **Pass**, **Not Applicable**, or **Blocker**. A blocker stops release; it is not a suggestion wearing a clipboard.

| Review area | Pass condition | Blocker examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Original editorial contribution | The episode has an original thesis, original script, and meaningful original analysis or visual explanation. | Generic narration over untransformed stock, a stitched compilation, or a script derived from another creator’s work. |
| Fact integrity | Material claims appear in the source log with correct date, scope, and wording. | Unsupported revenues/margins, a number without a period, or an inference narrated as a fact. |
| Rights and assets | Every commercial asset has a link, creator/vendor, license/permission status, use scope, and stored evidence. | Downloaded/ripped YouTube footage, unverified music, or a missing rights record. |
| Voice consent | Narration is owned, authorized, or properly licensed. | Unconsented voice clone, unclear commercial AI-voice terms, or unreviewed pronunciation. |
| Packaging truthfulness | Title and thumbnail accurately describe the delivered argument. | Fake scandal, inflated financial promise, or revealing a conclusion the video cannot support. |
| Platform readiness | Captions, chapters, playlist, end screen, sources, and disclosures are complete. | No captions, a misleading affiliate disclosure, or an end screen leading nowhere. |
| Human sign-off | Named reviewer watched the final exported file. | Publishing directly from an unreviewed render or auto-scheduling public release. |

## 2. Asset-Use Decision Tree

1. **Can the idea be explained with an original chart, map, diagram, typography sequence, or commissioned illustration?** Use that first.
2. **If a real-world visual is needed, do we own it, license it, have permission, or have a clear public-domain basis?** Record evidence before the edit.
3. **If the asset is an archival photo, logo, screenshot, headline, or short excerpt, is its use demonstrably tied to critique, commentary, reporting, or analysis?** Document purpose; do not assume this equals permission.
4. **If the answer is unclear, remove the asset or acquire a clearer alternative.** A dramatic three-second clip is not worth a rights argument with administrative qualities.

> Commentary and transformation can matter in legal analysis in some jurisdictions, but they are not automatic permission. Use licensed, owned, commissioned, permissioned, or verified public-domain material as the operational default.

Pexels’ license allows free use and modification under its published conditions but places restrictions around subjects, trademarks, endorsement, and redistribution. It is a source to review per asset—not a magic “everything is fine” button. [1]

## 3. Claim-Review Rules

| Narration pattern | Use when | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “According to [source], …” | A source directly reports a fact. | “According to Costco’s 2025 results release, membership fees were $5.323 billion in fiscal 2025.” |
| “The company says …” | Reporting a corporate claim or management framing. | “McDonald’s says its conventional franchise model combines its real-estate position with franchisee investment.” |
| “This suggests …” | Drawing a transparent editorial inference from sourced facts. | “This suggests the membership relationship gives Costco more than an ordinary transaction margin.” |
| “It is not the only explanation …” | The mechanism is material but incomplete. | “It is not the only explanation: convenience, location, and product quality still matter.” |
| “As of [date/period] …” | A figure, facility count, market share, or policy may change. | “At fiscal year-end 2025, Costco reported 914 warehouses.” |

## 5. Monetization-Safety Standard

YouTube’s channel monetization policies require content to be original and authentic and restrict content that is repetitive, mass-produced, or merely reused without meaningful transformation. Review can consider a channel’s main theme, newest and most-viewed videos, watch-time sources, metadata, and About section. [2] The practical response is not to memorize policy language; it is to preserve evidence of a real editorial process.

| Maintain this record | Why it matters operationally |
| --- | --- |
| Topic brief and original script history | Demonstrates an authored thesis and production process. |
| Source and claim log | Allows factual correction and supports due diligence. |
| Asset log and license evidence | Makes rights review possible after publication. |
| Original diagrams and editable project files | Shows visual transformation beyond decorative stock. |
| Final preflight and named approver | Establishes human accountability. |
| Retrospective notes | Creates a quality loop, not a content farm. |
