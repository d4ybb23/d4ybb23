# Channel Setup and Publishing SOP

**Applies to:** Channel creation, homepage setup, every long-form upload, and every post-publication observation cycle.  
**Public action boundary:** The prepared copy below may be used by the account owner; no channel is created or upload is made from this package.

## 1. Channel Setup Copy

| Field | Approved working copy |
| --- | --- |
| Channel name | **The Profit Map** |
| Handle target | `@theprofitmap` or the closest clear, non-confusing variation after availability and trademark review. |
| Banner line | **The hidden business logic behind the things you use.** |
| About description | **The Profit Map explains the hidden business logic behind the products, brands, and systems that shape everyday life. From retail and logistics to technology and famous company turnarounds, each episode uses research-led storytelling, original diagrams, and clear narration to reveal how the system really works. New business documentaries weekly.** |
| Contact policy | Use a dedicated business email, not a personal inbox. Record it in the channel operations vault, not this public repository. |
| Trailer premise | 30–45 seconds: three familiar questions, a two-sentence channel promise, then a montage of original diagrams, warehouses, maps, products, and headlines. No prolonged logo animation. |

## 3. Title Rules

One subject. One tension. Plain English. No claim should be more aggressive than the actual conclusion. A title does not need to be timid, but it does need to survive the first 75 seconds of the video without calling its attorney.

| Preferred structure | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Why [company/system] actually makes money | Why Costco Actually Makes Money by Selling So Little |
| How [company] turned [constraint] into a moat | How IKEA Turned a Flat Box Into a Global Moat |
| The hidden economics of [everyday thing] | The Hidden Economics of Self-Checkout |
| Why [industry behavior] exists | Why Airlines Sell Seats They Do Not Have |
| The mistake that broke [company] | The Mistake That Broke Borders |
| How [system] changed [real-world outcome] | How the Shipping Container Changed the Price of Everything |

## 4. Thumbnail Rules

| Requirement | Standard |
| --- | --- |
| Mobile legibility | View at approximately 10% scale; one focal idea must remain obvious. |
| On-image text | Zero to four words. It complements—never repeats—the title. |
| Brand language | Deep navy/charcoal base, warm off-white text, one topic-specific accent, high-contrast subject. |
| Visual object | One recognizable object, logo, place, person, or original diagram only where editorially relevant. |
| Truth test | It asks the video’s real question; it does not promise a scandal, guaranteed payoff, or secret revelation unsupported by the episode. |
| Variation | Generate 2–3 concepts before the editor locks final composition. |

Tags should be limited to useful exact-entity variants and likely spelling differences. YouTube explains that titles, thumbnails, and descriptions have a larger discovery role than tags, which are principally helpful for commonly misspelled terms. [1]

## 6. Upload Steps

1. Export a review master and watch it in full outside the edit timeline.
2. Complete the quality and rights gate.
3. Upload as **private** or **unlisted** for processing and final checks; never auto-public from the edit export.
4. Add the title, selected thumbnail, description, chapters, playlist, captions, and end screen.
5. Add one contextual pinned comment that invites a substantive response and links to the closest related episode when one exists.
6. Verify the mobile thumbnail, captions, end screen placement, cards, volume, and source links.
7. Schedule or publish only after named account-owner approval.
8. Start the 48-hour observation note. Do not make impulsive packaging changes based on trivial early movements.

## 7. Post-Release Observation

YouTube cautions creators to interpret click-through rate in context, including traffic sources and audience differences, rather than react to small movements or compare unlike tests. [2] The channel should monitor early signals calmly and convert them into one focused improvement experiment.

| Signal | Likely interpretation | Next experiment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Low clicks; solid early retention | The episode likely delivers better than the package communicates. | Test a more specific title or a clearer visual question after meaningful impressions. |
| High clicks; weak first-minute retention | The opening or package is misaligned. | Move the genuine answer or surprise earlier in the next script. |
| Strong search traffic | The question maps to a durable query. | Create an adjacent evergreen episode and playlist link. |
| Strong browse/suggested | The packaging and satisfaction likely match a wider audience. | Recreate the mechanism—not necessarily the subject. |
| Repeated ending drop | The conclusion/CTA is too slow or generic. | Bridge to the next video sooner and trim the farewell. |
