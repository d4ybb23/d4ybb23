## Slide 8 — The Long-Term Monetization Ladder

**Title:** Build the public asset without contaminating the editorial promise

| Stage | Mechanism | Entry condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | Authority-film services | Production capability and a qualified client need. |
| 1 | Relevant affiliates | Honest tool fit and clear material-connection disclosures. |
| 2 | Sponsor slots | Audience evidence, category fit, and strict editorial independence. |
| 3 | YouTube Partner Program | 1,000 subscribers plus 4,000 qualified public watch hours in the last 12 months, or 10 million qualified Shorts views in 90 days; channel review follows. [1] |
| 4 | Owned IP | Repeated demand for briefings, research products, memberships, or workshops. |

**Compliance note:** YouTube requires original and authentic content; it may review the channel’s theme, most-viewed/newest videos, metadata, and About section. [2]

## Slide 9 — Trust Is the Moat

**Title:** The money only works if the viewer believes the work

| Commercial rule | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| No paid editorial conclusion | Sponsors cannot buy the thesis or hide a material counterargument. |
| No fake ad-bumper | Third-party promotions cannot mimic YouTube ad formats where comparable formats are available. [3] |
| Clear sponsorship / affiliate disclosures | Paid or material relationships should be hard to miss; FTC guidance recommends in-video and description disclosure for video endorsements. [4] |
| Source, asset, and approval records | Protects factual integrity, rights, and monetization review. |

**Visual direction:** A simple gold “trust gate” flow that all revenue mechanisms must pass before publication.
