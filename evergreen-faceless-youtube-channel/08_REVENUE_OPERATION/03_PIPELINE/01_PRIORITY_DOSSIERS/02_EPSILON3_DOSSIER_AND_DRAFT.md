## 1. Why Epsilon3 Is a High-Fit Prospect

Epsilon3’s public narrative has a rare structural advantage: it can show how static instructions become an execution system for high-stakes operations. Its product site positions the company around process and resource management for advanced engineering, manufacturing, testing, and operations; it describes modules for creating and running procedures, managing parts and resources, planning work, and analyzing operational data. [1] [2]

The company also presents a current product-story moment. Its June 2026 changelog announced Epsilon3 2.0 and Epsilon3 Connect, framed as an extension of the digital thread across the complex-hardware lifecycle and supply chain. [3] This gives the sales conversation a focused commercial question: can a documentary-style mechanism story make the “digital thread” understandable to buyers and partners without revealing proprietary or export-controlled detail?

## 2. Story Hypothesis

> **Working title:** *The Procedure Is Not the Plan: How High-Stakes Teams Turn Instructions into Execution*

The story begins with a familiar misconception: a procedure looks like paperwork. In complex operations, it is instead a live control surface connecting requirements, roles, sign-offs, resources, execution history, issues, and learning. The narrative would move from the cost of disconnected documents and point tools to the business logic of versioned, collaborative procedure execution. The payoff is not “more software.” It is the ability to make complex work repeatable without pretending that complexity has vanished.
