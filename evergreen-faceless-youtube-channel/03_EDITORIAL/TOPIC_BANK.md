# Editorial Topic Bank — First 40 Episodes

**Status:** Ranked working slate  
**Scoring rule:** Score before researching; rescore only when the dossier changes the thesis.  
**Use:** Choose the next video by strategic fit and evidence availability, not by whichever company logo is presently behaving the most photogenically.

## Scoring Model

Each topic is evaluated on a ten-point scale. The total is weighted to prioritize a good question and a durable library over speculative CPM lore.

| Criterion | Weight | What a 10 means |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Viewer tension | 25% | A familiar subject contains a clear, surprising contradiction. |
| Evergreen depth | 20% | The argument remains relevant beyond a news cycle. |
| Evidence quality | 20% | Primary sources and credible secondary sources are accessible. |
| Visual explanation potential | 15% | Original diagrams, maps, timelines, or physical process visuals can carry meaning. |
| Channel fit | 10% | Reinforces The Profit Map’s promise and pillar balance. |
| Production efficiency | 10% | Achievable at launch without exotic licensing, travel, or special access. |

> **Qualification gate:** A score does not authorize production. A topic advances only when its one-sentence thesis is non-obvious, its consequential claims have credible source leads, and its title can make an honest promise.

## Decision Procedure Before Research

1. Copy a row into the topic-brief template.
2. Write the answer thesis in one sentence before searching.
3. Find at least two primary or authoritative sources and one credible counterpoint or complication.
4. List the original explanatory visuals before commissioning footage.
5. Kill the topic if the explanation is either obvious, speculative, or dependent on an impossible-to-license clip.

No topic is sacred. The audience owes us attention, not sentimental loyalty to a title that was very exciting in a spreadsheet three Thursdays ago.
