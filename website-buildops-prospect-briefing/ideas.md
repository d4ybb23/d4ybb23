# BuildOps Prospect Briefing — Design Directions

## Three Possible Directions

### 1. Signal Room
**Very Brief Intro:** An editorial research briefing that feels like a field report assembled by people who understand both machinery and margins. It gives the prospect space to think rather than trying to sell them confetti.

**Probability:** 0.04

### 2. Drafting Table
**Very Brief Intro:** A warm, tactile systems brief inspired by engineering notebooks, grid paper, and specification sheets. It treats operational complexity as something to be made legible, not decorated.

**Probability:** 0.07

### 3. Control Surface
**Very Brief Intro:** A dark, cinematic command-center experience using live-status motifs and restrained luminous accents. It would make the operating-system idea feel immediate and technical.

**Probability:** 0.02

## Selected Direction: Signal Room

### Design Movement

**Swiss information design meets industrial wayfinding and contemporary systems journalism.** The site should feel like a well-prepared briefing delivered before a serious conversation: precise, composed, and specific enough to signal effort without pretending to be BuildOps’ own website.

### Core Principles

1. **Evidence before ornament.** Every visual move should help separate observed public facts, a reasoned story hypothesis, and a proposed next step.
2. **Asymmetric clarity.** A persistent report index and a narrow reading column create a deliberate, non-landing-page structure.
3. **Operational texture.** Thin rules, coordinate labels, source markers, and time stamps make the page feel assembled from a real research process.
4. **Commercial restraint.** No inflated claims, fake dashboards, conversion counters, client logos, or simulated relationship signals.

### Color Philosophy

The base is **warm drafting paper** rather than generic white, representing unfinished but considered work. Dense graphite provides quiet authority; **signal vermilion** marks hypotheses, attention points, and action boundaries. A subdued mineral blue supports source links and operational references. The red is a controlled signal color, never a decoration flood.

### Layout Paradigm

The page behaves like a vertically unfolding field note. A left-side index rail anchors the reader on desktop; the content moves through uneven editorial bands, including a split mechanism map, a long-form claim sequence, and a narrow evidence ledger. Mobile collapses the rail into a compact dossier label above the story.

### Signature Elements

1. A numbered **FIELD NOTE 01** stamp with a clipped-corner bracket.
2. Fine **route-line diagrams** that connect the field, office, finance, and customer handoffs.
3. Small **Evidence / Hypothesis / Boundary** chips that classify each significant statement.

### Interaction Philosophy

Interaction should feel like handling a concise briefing document. The index scrolls to sections, source markers open official public pages in a new tab, and the mechanism map reveals short annotations on hover or focus. There is no form, no fake calendar, and no faux data visualization.

### Animation

Use only a gentle 180–240ms opacity/transform entrance for in-flow sections, with an 45ms stagger, gated by reduced-motion preferences. Index active-state changes and source-link underlines use short transitions. No looping, bouncing, parallax, or decorative “data” movement.

### Typography System

Use **Space Grotesk** for headlines, labels, and navigation: compact, technical, and slightly industrial. Use **Source Serif 4** for narrative passages so the story reads like editorial analysis rather than application UI. Use **IBM Plex Mono** for timestamps, evidence labels, identifiers, and citations. Headline scale is dramatic but left-aligned; labels remain compact and measured.

### Brand Essence

**A research-led authority-film concept for companies whose operating advantage is easier to demonstrate than to explain.** Personality: **exact, curious, composed**.

### Brand Voice

Headlines state the mechanism, not the aspiration. CTAs invite review, not artificial urgency. Microcopy distinguishes observed evidence from proposed work.

Examples:

> “The field-to-invoice story hiding inside one connected operating record.”

> “This is a research draft, not a claim of relationship.”

### Wordmark & Logo

Use a small custom text lockup: **EVOLVE HUMAN / FIELD NOTE** separated by a square registration mark. The mark is a compact four-node route map—field, office, finance, customer—drawn as a neutral graphic rather than a BuildOps logo.

### Signature Brand Color

**Signal Vermilion — `#E3472F`**. This color appears only where the reader must distinguish a hypothesis, a decision point, or a boundary.

## Style Decisions

- The site is an **unsolicited independent prospect briefing** prepared by Evolve Human. It must never imply BuildOps sponsorship, approval, partnership, or client status.
- Public-source observations, the authority-film hypothesis, and commercial next steps must remain visually and verbally distinct.
- Use original generated visuals and abstract system diagrams only; do not use BuildOps’ marks, customer logos, product screenshots, or jobsite imagery without an explicit right to do so.
- Each Field Note preserves the dossier structure but reshapes the route-line motif and visual rhythm around the prospect’s actual operating mechanism: flow for BuildOps, decision memory for Pirros, and procedure control for Epsilon3.
- The four-node route mark is the compact authorship signature. It recurs in headers, footers, stamps, route maps, and briefing transitions.
- The taxonomy is intentional and stable: blue-backed markers signal **observed evidence**; vermilion dashed markers signal a **proposed narrative lens**; the boundary section signals what is not authorized, represented, or promised.
